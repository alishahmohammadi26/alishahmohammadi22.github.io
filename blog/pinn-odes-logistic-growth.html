Can a Logistic PINN Forecast U.S. Population 86 Years Ahead?

A leakage-free study of why a PINN can solve the logistic equation correctly and still forecast the population incorrectly—and what adaptive sampling, DeepONet, and a stronger PINN formulation can actually contribute.

PINN Case Study Intermediate ·Jul 2026 ·24 min read

01 Start with the forecast origin

This is really three different forecasting questions

The first mistake in long-range forecasting is often made before the model is built: the forecast origin is left ambiguous. A model can only use information that existed at the date from which it claims to predict. Once that rule is explicit, the population problem separates into three experiments.

The leakage rule

Using 2025 data to forecast 2026 is correct. Using 2025 data to select a model that is presented as a 2014 forecast is not. The same year can be legal in one experiment and leakage in another because the forecast origins differ.

02 Experiment A

What 1790–1940 can—and cannot—predict

The fixed logistic model assumes that the population follows

$$\frac{dP}{dt}=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K}\right),$$

with one constant growth rate \(r\) and one constant carrying capacity \(K\). Because this ODE has an exact solution, the exact curve must be fitted before a PINN is introduced. Otherwise, the neural network may receive credit simply for approximating a solution already available analytically.

The 16 observations from 1790 through 1940 identify a carrying capacity near 186 million. That value makes the model look plausible inside the calibration period but forces the future curve to saturate near a level the real population later exceeds.

This experiment supports only a counterfactual statement:

Counterfactual, not forecast

“What would happen if the logistic regime inferred before 1940 continued unchanged?” is a valid mathematical question. It is not the same as predicting the population that actually occurs through 2026.

03 PINN versus governing law

Why the PINN and adaptive sampling do not fix the forecast

The standard PINN minimizes a data loss and a logistic residual over the full extrapolation domain:

$$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{data}+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{d\hat P}{dt}-r\hat P\left(1-\frac{\hat P}{K}\right)\right)^2\right].$$

The residual-adaptive version begins with uniform collocation points, evaluates the residual over a dense candidate grid, adds points where the residual is largest, and retrains. This is a useful way to ask where the network should enforce the ODE more carefully.

RAR reduced the scaled residual by approximately 17.6%, yet the population forecast did not improve. That is not a contradiction. It means the adaptive model solved the assumed law more accurately while the law itself remained inadequate for the unseen regime.

Three tests, three meanings

Training error tests interpolation. Physics residual tests whether the network follows the chosen equation. Post-cutoff error tests forecasting. A PINN can pass the first two and fail the third.

04 Experiment B

An honest forecast beginning in 2014

For the modern experiment, the clock stops at the end of 2013. Annual values through 2013 are visible. The 2014–2025 observations are locked away. The 2026 value is forecast-only.

Model and history-window choices are made with rolling-origin validation. The notebook trains through 1970 and predicts the next 13 years, then repeats from 1975, 1980, and so on through a final origin of 2000. The last validation target is 2013, so the final holdout remains untouched.

temporal_selection.pyPseudocode
for origin in [1970, 1975, ..., 2000]:
    train = population.loc[:origin]
    forecast = model.fit(train).predict(origin + 1, origin + 13)
    validation_errors.extend(forecast - observed_future)

select_model_and_window(validation_errors)
refit_through_2013()
open_2014_to_2025_holdout_once()

The strict pre-2014 winner was an all-history Gompertz curve. It later scored 9.42M RMSE on 2014–2025. The final period behaved differently from the older backtests that selected it.

A fixed recent-model median ensemble scored approximately 2.04M RMSE. It did not assume that one curve would remain best across every regime.

05 Data-window diagnosis

Why “use all the data” can be the wrong answer

More observations reduce variance only when the observations remain relevant to the same underlying relationship. With a changing process, distant history can dominate the fitted parameters and pull the model away from the regime immediately preceding the forecast origin.

Latest 10 years0.901MBest ex-post diagnostic for this particular holdout
Latest 20 years0.940MNearly as accurate, with a longer local history
All annual history7.449MOlder regimes distort the 2013-origin parameters
Do not turn hindsight into selection

The 10-year logistic curve is the best model after the 2014–2025 answers are revealed. It is valuable evidence that recent data matter, but it cannot be presented as an unbiased choice that a forecaster definitely would have made in 2013.

The right use of old data is not necessarily to give every old observation equal weight in the final fit. Old periods are also valuable for backtesting, detecting regime instability, and rejecting models whose parameters do not transport across time.

06 Operator learning

Can DeepONet help with one national trajectory?

DeepONet learns a mapping from an input function to an output function. In this experiment, the branch network receives a historical log-population window and the trunk network receives the requested forecast horizon. Their inner product predicts cumulative future growth.

The implementation creates more than a thousand history–horizon pairs, but those pairs are overlapping fragments of one U.S. series. A window ending in 1999 and another ending in 2000 share almost every observation. The row count therefore overstates the number of independent systems the operator has seen.

DeepONet becomes more defensible when training contains genuinely different trajectories: multiple regions, multiple countries, or a large independent library of dynamical systems with different coefficient functions. Operator learning needs variation across input functions, not only many slices of one function.

07 The next PINN experiment

Replace the fixed law with a regime-aware inverse PINN

The failure points directly to a more relevant PINN question. Instead of assuming that one \(r\) and one \(K\) remain constant for centuries, allow the effective coefficients to change smoothly:

$$\frac{dP}{dt}=r_\phi(t)P\left(1-\frac{P}{K_\psi(t)}\right),\qquad r_\phi(t)>0,\quad K_\psi(t)>P(t).$$

This is an inverse problem: the population network and the coefficient networks are learned jointly. The additional flexibility must be controlled, or the coefficient networks can explain any smooth curve without learning a transportable relationship.

A defensible loss would include data fit, ODE residual, smoothness penalties, and an anchor at the forecast origin:

$$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{data}+\lambda_{phys}\mathcal{L}_{ODE}+\lambda_r\left\|\frac{dr_\phi}{dt}\right\|_2^2+\lambda_K\left\|\frac{dK_\psi}{dt}\right\|_2^2+\lambda_{anchor}\mathcal{L}_{origin}.$$

A research direction, not a declared winner

The regime-aware PINN has not earned a performance claim merely because it is more flexible. It must beat the exact logistic model, fixed-logistic PINNs, and recent-window ensemble on untouched forecast origins while producing coefficient functions that remain stable across nearby backtests.

09 Decision guide

What the study actually establishes

QuestionAnswer
Can 1790–1940 predict the real population through 2026?No—not under one constant logistic law. It produces a historical counterfactual, not a credible observed-path forecast.
Did the PINN fail numerically?No. It reproduced the assumed logistic law. The governing assumption failed to transport.
Can adaptive sampling repair the problem?No. It can improve residual enforcement, but it cannot add missing regime information.
Should all historical observations receive equal weight?Not automatically. Use old periods for backtesting and stability tests; compare recent windows for the final fit.
Can DeepONet help?Not from overlapping windows of one aggregate trajectory. It needs genuinely diverse input-output functions.
What should a 2014-origin study use?Annual data through 2013, rolling-origin selection ending before 2014, a locked 2014–2025 holdout, and an ensemble benchmark.
What PINN should be tested next?A regularized regime-aware inverse PINN with time-varying coefficients, adaptive sampling after structural validation, and multi-seed uncertainty.
The lasting lesson

Physics-informed learning is strongest when the physics contains information that remains valid outside the observations. When the law itself changes, the research task is not to optimize the same residual harder. It is to formulate a better falsifiable law and test it without looking into the future.
